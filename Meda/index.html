Meda Dhatu Overview

Ayurveda Science
Adipose Tissue System (Meda)

In Ayurvedic medicine, Meda Dhatu represents the lipid and adipose tissue system responsible for bodily lubrication (Snehana), thermal insulation, energy storage, and physical cushioning for internal organs and skeletal structures.

18-24% Normal Mass Index
Jala + Prithvi Mahabhuta Dominance
Snayu & Vasa Associated Upadhatu
Sveda (Sweat) Metabolic Waste
Primary Functions (Karma)
  • Snehana: Provides systemic unctuousness and joint lubrication.
  • Dharana: Gives shape, firmness, and structural padding to muscles.
  • Sveda Utpatti: Regulates body temperature via thermogenesis and perspiration.
  • Asthi Poshana: Serves as the precursor tissue nourishment for Asthi Dhatu (bone).
Microscopic Adipose Lobules

Subcutaneous fat is organized into distinct adipose lobules separated by fibrous connective tissue septa. Adipocytes store triacylglycerols in large lipid droplets, imparting a golden-yellow hue due to naturally dissolved carotenoids.

Lobulated Septa Subsurface Scattering Warm Amber Lipid Droplets
Pathological States
Meda Vriddhi (Hyper-adiposity): Excessive accumulation causing breathlessness, lethargy, flaccidity, and metabolic imbalance (Stholya / Obesity).
Meda Kshaya (Depletion): Loss of subcutaneous cushion, joint stiffness, dryness of skin and eyes, splenic enlargement, and rib prominence.
